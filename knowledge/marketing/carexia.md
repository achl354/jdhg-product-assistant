# Carexia FPVE — Treatment Chair

**Content type: marketing/sales collateral (JDHG flyer), not a manufacturer IFU or ARTG/regulatory document.** Source: JDHG Flyer - Carexia FPVE - JDHG121124.pdf — note the source PDF itself carries two different date codes on different pages (JDHG121124 on the main flyer, JDHG140524 on the specifications page), unreconciled; treat the document date as approximate.

## What it is

Carexia FPVE electrically powered treatment recliner chair, for Ambulatory Care Departments, Outpatient Clinics, Oncology, and Dialysis Units. Top-of-range model with both electric height adjustment and electrically operated profiling functions.

## Features
- Robust epoxy-coated steel frame with ABS plastic base cover (aesthetics + easier cleaning)
- Ergonomically contoured seat, leg rest (short, standard) and backrest cushions with integrated headrest
- Width-adjustable Comfort armrests standard (Anatomical armrests available as alternative)
- Leg rest movement synchronised with backrest and Trendelenburg functions (when backrest angle is below 60°)
- Quality faux leather upholstery; upholstered/contoured chair sides
- Two IV pole receptacles; central-locking twin castors; under-seat storage (rear access); push bar behind backrest
- Electrically height adjustable, electrically operated backrest, leg rest, and Trendelenburg; hand control pendant; pre-programmed "one touch" buttons for Trendelenburg and Recliner functions
- **Safe Working Load: 200kg in standard chair profile.** A reduced limit of 135kg also appears in the source flyer — see the NEEDS CONFIRMATION note below before quoting it.

## Safe Working Load reduction — NEEDS CONFIRMATION
The source flyer states 200kg SWL for the chair profile, then gives a reduced **135kg** figure in two places worded differently: the feature bullet list says "135kg in Trendelenburg position," while the specification table separately labels the same number "Safe working load (fully reclined)." Trendelenburg tilt (the whole chair angled head-down) and the backrest being fully reclined (the backrest's own recline angle, listed separately in this table at "~85° to 6°") are not necessarily the same physical state — a chair could plausibly be in Trendelenburg without the backrest being fully reclined, or vice versa. The source document does not clarify whether the 135kg limit applies specifically to Trendelenburg tilt, specifically to a fully-reclined backrest, or to both simultaneously. **Do not tell a rep or customer that 135kg is "the Trendelenburg limit" or "the fully-reclined limit" as a settled fact — say the reduced limit is 135kg under at least one of these conditions per JDHG's flyer, and that the exact condition needs to be confirmed with JDHG/Regulatory before being quoted precisely.**
