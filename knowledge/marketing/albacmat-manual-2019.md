# AlbacMat — Manufacturer User Manual (2019)

**Source: AlbacMat Manual 2019.pdf, manufacturer (Albac Enterprises Pty Ltd) user manual. Content type: closer to IFU-grade than the rest of the marketing/ folder, but dated 2019 with no confirmation that a newer manufacturer edition doesn't exist — treat `current_or_superseded` as needs_confirmation, not confirmed-current.** See `marketing/albacmat-flyer.md` for JDHG's current sales flyer, and `knowledge/sources.meta.json` for full source metadata.

## Design & materials (per this manual)
- Corrugated polypropylene board base — slides easily over carpet (incl. Flotex/Uger), lino, timber, and outdoor surfaces (gravel, bitumen, concrete); crushing point 4.92 kgf/cm²
- Rolls up to 30cm x 7cm for storage; fluorescent strips and instruction label for use by untrained bystanders
- 3 sets of Velcro straps (torso; hips, or 1+2 can form an X over the torso) plus a foot holder to keep feet contained during transit, especially on stairs
- Independently tested by CSIRO (Textiles division): stitching breaking point >500kg; very slow/no ignition in flammability testing
- Meets AS 2755.2 and AS 2001.2.3.2, CE marked (Class 1)

## Safe Working Load — NEEDS CONFIRMATION
**This manual states a 500kg Safe Working Load, with a separately tested safe load of 160kg.** JDHG's current flyer (`marketing/albacmat-flyer.md`) states 470kg SWL instead. The manual itself explains the 500/160kg distinction as: "The AlbacMat has a Safe Working Load of 500kg. This is beyond any human limits of manual transfer and therefore the SWL is reliant upon the rescuer's own capability" — i.e. 500kg is a material/construction rating, and 160kg was the practical load used during testing, not necessarily a second official limit. Neither of those two manual-stated numbers matches the flyer's single 470kg figure, and the source documents don't reconcile this. **Confirm the current authoritative SWL with JDHG/Regulatory before quoting any of these three numbers to a customer.**

## Range (per this manual — some variants may not appear on the current flyer)
- **AlbacMat Rescue Mat (standard)** — paediatric-adaptable
- **AlbacMat Large** — carry handles each side, carried by up to 8 people, suited to outdoor rescue and non-slip indoor flooring (e.g. operating theatres)
- **AlbacMat Holder** — stores 4 AlbacMats of either size
- **AlbacMat with Thermal Cover** — larger at the torso, 3 carry handles per side, thermal cover; mining-sector origin, also used in hospital theatre rooms
- **Bariatric size** — based on the Large, wider base board — contact your local distributor for current availability

## Use technique
- Correct posture: step forward first (raises the patient into position with straight back, bent elbows); stay close to the mat (gravity/leverage)
- Single-person operation is standard; two-person technique recommended for larger/difficult patients or stairs
- **Stairs — descending:** single-person method uses the under-handles, rescuer walks backward-angled down the stairs (patient travels backward, resting against the rescuer's thigh for balance). Two-person method: one person stationed on the stairs guides the patient's feet/body onto the steps using the foot-pocket handle (not the strap), the other guides from the head end using the longer strap, letting the mat glide over each step.
- **Stairs — ascending:** possible but not ideal; best performed by two people going straight up, facing the stairs (backs to the patient), using the side handles at the head end, with the patient's torso raised to near-sitting for comfort and to minimise the bottom resting on each step.
- Two-person rescue: each person faces forward, one hand on the mat's under-handle, outside hand free; both step toward the back of the mat first for correct posture.

## Maintenance
- Unroll and inspect every 6 months: check strap positions, stitching for weakness, board grazing/suitability
- Clean with hose/cloth, avoid harsh chemicals; air-dry fully before re-rolling for storage
- If body fluid has contacted the strap webbing, clean under strict hygiene control or replace the unit
- After any evacuation use: inspect stitching (handles, straps, board surround) and the underside of the board for grazing/puncture damage. Minor grazing (smaller than a fist) or small punctures don't restrict performance; significant grazing means replace. Do not re-stitch a worn/broken unit — replace it.
- Roll up with the correct side down (rescuer at head end, facing lengthwise, roller's left) to avoid the Velcro straps joining on themselves in storage

## Markets referenced in this manual
Aged care/nursing homes, hospitals (especially theatre rooms, with the thermal-cover variant), mining, mortuary transfers, schools (esp. special-needs), cruise ships, emergency services (Metropolitan Ambulance Service, Country Fire Authority, and State Emergency Service branches in Victoria specifically named as using the thermal-cover variant), general public/home use.
